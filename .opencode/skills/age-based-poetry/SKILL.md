# Age-Based Poetry and Songwriting Skill

## Purpose

Create original, engaging, age-appropriate poems and songs based on the target audience's age, vocabulary, interests, emotional development, and attention span.

Adapt the language, rhythm, rhyme scheme, length, structure, and musical style to the requested age group.

## General Rules

1. Always identify the intended age group before writing.
2. If the age group is not provided, ask the user for it.
3. If the topic is missing, ask the user what the poem or song should be about.
4. Use original wording. Do not copy existing poems, song lyrics, or distinctive passages from published works.
5. Use vocabulary and sentence structures suitable for the intended age.
6. Make the writing engaging, memorable, and easy to understand.
7. Choose a rhythm and rhyme scheme appropriate to the age group.
8. Avoid unnecessarily complicated words for younger children.
9. Use positive, inclusive, age-appropriate themes.
10. Follow any requested topic, mood, length, language, or writing style unless it conflicts with these instructions.

## Age Group 1: Babies and Toddlers (0–3 Years)

### Writing Style

* Write gentle lullabies, nursery-style verses, and soothing rhythmic phrases.
* Use very simple words and short lines.
* Use repetition, comforting sounds, and predictable rhythms.
* Prefer easy rhymes and familiar objects, animals, nature, and bedtime themes.
* Create a calming, affectionate, and reassuring mood.
* Make the words easy for parents or carers to sing aloud.

### Structure

* Usually 2–4 short verses or a brief repeated refrain.
* Keep each line very short.
* Repetition is encouraged.

### Suitable Themes

Sleep, love, family, stars, moonlight, animals, cuddles, and everyday discoveries.

## Age Group 2: Young Children (4–6 Years)

### Writing Style

* Write short, playful poems with simple rhyming words.
* Use familiar vocabulary and concrete imagery.
* Include fun characters, animals, imagination, and everyday adventures.
* Use repetition, sound patterns, and a cheerful rhythm.
* Make the poem easy to memorise and recite aloud.

### Rhyme Schemes

Prefer simple patterns such as:

* AABB: consecutive pairs of rhyming lines.
* ABAB: alternating rhyming lines.

### Structure

* Usually 2–4 short verses.
* Use approximately 4 lines per verse.
* Keep the meaning clear and the rhythm natural.

### Suitable Themes

Friendship, colours, animals, family, sharing, nature, play, and discovering the world.

## Age Group 3: Children (7–10 Years)

### Writing Style

* Write more developed poems with richer vocabulary and vivid imagery.
* Introduce interesting comparisons, metaphors, personification, and storytelling.
* Explore themes such as courage, kindness, curiosity, confidence, friendship, and personal growth.
* Use varied sentence structures while keeping the meaning accessible.
* Create a satisfying rhythm and a consistent or deliberately varied rhyme scheme.

### Rhyme Schemes

Choose a suitable scheme for the poem, such as:

* AABB: paired rhymes.
* ABAB: alternating rhymes.
* ABBA: enclosed rhymes.
* ABCB: the second and fourth lines rhyme.
* A combination of schemes across different verses.

Do not force a rhyme if it makes a line sound unnatural.

### Structure

* Usually 3–6 verses, depending on the request.
* Use approximately 4–8 lines per verse.
* Develop an idea, story, or emotional journey across the poem.
* Vary the rhythm and verse structure when it improves the writing.

### Suitable Themes

Adventure, imagination, perseverance, teamwork, nature, learning, dreams, and believing in oneself.

## Age Group 4: Older Children and Teenagers (11 Years and Above)

### Writing Style

* When requested, write original pop-song lyrics rather than conventional children's poetry.
* Use contemporary, age-appropriate language that sounds natural when sung.
* Explore relatable emotions, friendships, identity, ambition, confidence, change, belonging, and personal growth.
* Use memorable hooks, rhythmic phrasing, repetition, and a strong central message.
* Balance expressive language with clarity and authenticity.
* Avoid sounding childish or forcing slang.

### Song Structure

Choose an appropriate structure, such as:

* Verse 1
* Pre-Chorus
* Chorus
* Verse 2
* Pre-Chorus
* Chorus
* Bridge
* Final Chorus

Shorter songs may use fewer sections.

### Songwriting Techniques

* Create a memorable, original chorus.
* Use internal rhyme, end rhyme, repetition, and rhythmic variation where appropriate.
* Make lines comfortable to sing aloud.
* Build emotional development from the verses to the chorus.
* Make the bridge introduce a fresh perspective or emotional turning point.
* Do not force every line to rhyme.

### Suitable Themes

Self-belief, dreams, friendship, independence, overcoming challenges, belonging, hope, and discovering one's identity.

## Choosing the Right Format

When the user supplies an age and topic:

1. Select the relevant age group.
2. Choose the appropriate format: lullaby, short rhyming poem, structured poem, or pop song.
3. Select a suitable length, rhythm, rhyme scheme, and vocabulary level.
4. Write the complete original piece.
5. Check that the language, structure, and themes are appropriate for the intended audience.
6. Revise awkward rhymes and unnatural phrasing.
7. Present the finished poem or song with a suitable title.

If the user requests a particular form, such as a sonnet, free verse, or a specific pop-song structure, honour that request where appropriate.

## Quality Checklist

Before presenting the final piece, check:

* Is the writing appropriate for the requested age?
* Does the vocabulary match the audience's understanding?
* Is the rhythm comfortable to read or sing aloud?
* Are the rhymes natural rather than forced?
* Does the structure suit the requested form?
* Is the message clear and engaging?
* Is the wording original?
* Have all requested themes and constraints been addressed?

## Example Requests

* "Write a lullaby about the moon for a two-year-old."
* "Write a short rhyming poem about friendship for a five-year-old."
* "Write a six-verse adventure poem with an ABAB rhyme scheme for a nine-year-old."
* "Write an uplifting pop song about confidence for a thirteen-year-old."
* "Write an original pop song for teenagers about chasing their dreams, with two verses, a chorus, and a bridge."

Use the age-specific guidance above to produce the most appropriate result.
